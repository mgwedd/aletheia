# Consent and Legal — Read This Before Recording

Aletheia makes it technically easy to record a video therapy session.
That doesn't make it automatically legal or ethical to do so. Before you
record a session with this app, you (the therapist) are responsible for
confirming, independently of this software:

1. **Client consent.** Most jurisdictions and licensing boards require
   informed, often written, consent from the client before a session is
   recorded — including sessions conducted over video call. Verbal
   agreement alone may not be sufficient depending on where you practice.
2. **Licensing board and legal requirements.** Requirements vary by state,
   country, and licensing body (and can differ between audio-only and
   audio+video recordings, and between recordings kept short-term vs.
   retained long-term). Check your board's current rules and your state's
   two-party/one-party consent laws for recorded communications.
3. **Storage and retention rules.** Even fully local, on-device storage may
   be subject to the same recordkeeping, retention, and eventual deletion
   requirements as any other clinical record.
4. **Third-party platform terms.** Some video call platforms restrict or
   prohibit recording through a separate, non-native tool (which is what
   this app is, since it captures system audio rather than integrating
   with any particular platform). Check the terms of whichever platform
   you use for sessions.

Aletheia does not supply your consent form, does not enforce any
particular jurisdiction's rules, and does not contact anyone on your
behalf. It does keep a record of the consent you attest to for each session
(below). Whether that consent is valid is your responsibility.

## What Aletheia records

Before it records or imports audio for a session that needs consent, Aletheia
opens a **Record consent** sheet. You enter:

- **Who consented:** the patient, the patient and others present, a parent or
  guardian, or another representative (with their name, if you wish).
- **How:** verbal or written.
- **When:** the date and time the consent was given.
- **The attestation:** "I obtained consent from everyone in this session to
  record it and to process the recording with AI." Recording stays disabled
  until you tick it.

This is a record of what you attest. Aletheia cannot check that anyone
actually agreed.

The record is saved with the session, and the activity log notes that consent
was attested (by session and record ID, not by name). It appears in every
export of the session as a "Recording consent" section, and in Export All
Records. Once the session's note is signed, its consent record is locked and
cannot be changed.

## First session or every session

In Settings > General, **Ask for recording consent** is either:

- **First session** (the default): you are asked once for each patient, and
  later sessions show "Consent on file since" that date.
- **Every session:** you are asked before every session.

You can set a policy for one patient in their profile, or for several at once
with **Apply to Patients…**; a patient's own choice comes before the Settings
default. Choose **Every session** whenever the people in the room may change
(a family member joins, an interpreter attends, a child's guardian changes),
or when your board or practice policy asks for consent each time. Consent
given by one person does not cover another who joins later.

## All-party consent: examples

Some states require every party to a conversation to consent before it is
recorded, not just one. If a session crosses state lines, the stricter rule is
the safer one to follow. These three statutes are examples, not a complete list;
many other states have their own rules, and the rules change:

- California Penal Code §632
- Washington RCW 9.73.030
- Connecticut Gen. Stat. §52-570d

Check the law in every state where you and your client are located during a
session, and your licensing board's rules.

## Suggested script before recording

Read something like this aloud, and wait for a clear answer before you press
record. Adapt it to your practice; this is a starting point, not legal advice.

> Before we start, I would like to record today's session so that I can write
> my clinical notes. The recording stays on my computer, where AI software
> turns it into a transcript and a draft of my notes. The recording is deleted
> after it is transcribed; the transcript and notes are kept in your record.
> Do you agree to be recorded? You can say no, and you can ask me to pause or
> stop at any time.

Then record the answer in the Record consent sheet, and in your own records
according to your practice's policy.

## What clients can ask for

Don't promise a client that a recording or transcript will be deleted on
request. Under HIPAA a client can see and get a copy of their records and ask
for corrections (amendments), and the law or your board may require you to
keep records for a set time. By default Aletheia deletes the audio after it is
transcribed, except when the transcript comes out blank (then the audio is
kept so nothing is lost) or you have chosen to keep recordings; the transcript
is kept as part of the record.

## Not supported: 42 CFR Part 2

Aletheia is not built for substance use disorder treatment records covered by
42 CFR Part 2, which has its own consent rules. If your practice is covered,
that is your responsibility.

## A note on AI summaries

AI-generated summaries (drafted by the app's built-in on-device model) are a drafting aid, not a clinical record of truth. They can
misstate, omit, or misattribute things said in a session. Always read a
summary alongside its transcript before relying on it, and treat the
transcript — not the summary — as the authoritative record.
